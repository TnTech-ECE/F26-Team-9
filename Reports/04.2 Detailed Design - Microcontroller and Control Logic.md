## Function of the Subsystem

This segment should elucidate the role of the subsystem within the entire system, detailing its intended function, aligned with the conceptual design.



The Microcontroller and Control Logic subsystem is responsible for processing operating measurements from the Lochinvar water heating system and determining the appropriate circulation pump speed based on the current heating demand. This subsystem serves as the main decision-making component of the proposed variable-speed pump controller. Its purpose is to reduce unnecessary electrical consumption while maintaining the temperature performance and minimum circulation flow required for proper boiler operation.

The circulation pump plays an important role in transferring heated water throughout the system. However, operating a circulation pump at a constant speed may result in unnecessary electrical consumption when the heating demand is lower than the maximum operating condition. The proposed controller addresses this limitation by allowing pump speed to change according to measured system conditions.

The controller will receive information from the water flow sensor, boiler firing-rate signal, inlet water temperature sensor, and outlet water temperature sensor. These measurements will be processed to determine the current operating condition and calculate a pump-speed command. The selected control concept uses water flow rate and boiler firing rate to establish an initial operating point. Outlet-temperature feedback is then used to determine whether an additional adjustment is necessary.

The microcontroller must also maintain the minimum water circulation rate required by the selected Lochinvar equipment. This requirement is important because reducing pump speed may decrease the flow rate through the boiler. Although lower pump speeds can reduce electrical consumption, operation below the manufacturer's minimum flow requirement could negatively affect heat transfer and equipment operation.

Another important function is maintaining acceptable outlet water temperature performance. The project specifies a steady-state temperature target of approximately ±4°F from the selected setpoint. The controller must therefore balance electrical efficiency with temperature stability instead of prioritizing pump-speed reduction alone.

The subsystem will also communicate operating information to the Data Logging and Simulation subsystem. This information will include the measured temperatures, water flow rate, boiler firing rate, pump-speed command, and controller status. The recorded measurements will support comparisons between baseline operation and the proposed variable-speed control strategy.

The controller is intended to operate alongside the existing boiler control system rather than replace the manufacturer's internal safety functions. Boiler combustion control, high-temperature protection, and other built-in protective functions will remain independent of the proposed controller.

Overall, the Microcontroller and Control Logic subsystem provides the processing and control functions necessary to adjust circulation pump speed according to heating demand while maintaining the required operating conditions of the water heating system.

### Functional Specifications
---

The controller shall acquire and process the measurements required for the proposed control algorithm. These measurements include water flow rate, boiler firing rate, inlet water temperature, and outlet water temperature.

Each measurement must be converted into an appropriate engineering unit before being used in the control calculation. Water flow rate will be represented in gallons per minute, temperature will be represented consistently in degrees Fahrenheit or degrees Celsius, and boiler firing rate will be represented as a percentage of maximum firing demand.

The controller shall calculate a pump-speed command using the measured operating conditions. The command must remain within the allowable operating range of the selected pump and motor controller.

The controller shall also make the relevant operating measurements and control values available to the Data Logging and Simulation subsystem.


### Temperature Regulation Requirements
---

The controller shall support the project's target of maintaining the outlet water temperature within ±4°F of the selected setpoint during steady-state operation, provided the heating equipment has sufficient capacity to satisfy the demand.

Temperature error will be calculated using:

[
e(t)=T_{\text{setpoint}}-T_{\text{outlet}}(t)
]

where:

(e(t)) is the temperature error.

(T_{\text{setpoint}}) is the desired outlet temperature.

(T_{\text{outlet}}(t)) is the measured outlet temperature.

The control algorithm will use this error to determine whether the initial pump-speed command requires adjustment.

The relationship between pump speed and outlet temperature must be established using simulation and experimental measurements. The direction of the required speed adjustment cannot be assumed to remain identical under every hydraulic and heating condition.


### Minimum Flow Requirements
---

The controller shall maintain the minimum circulation flow required by the selected Lochinvar boiler.

This requirement will act as an operating constraint on the pump-speed command. The controller must not intentionally reduce pump speed when doing so would cause the measured flow rate to fall below the applicable manufacturer requirement.

The minimum flow threshold must be obtained from the documentation for the specific boiler model and operating configuration.

A defined protective response must be implemented when the measured flow rate falls below the allowable minimum. The response must be compatible with the boiler and pump equipment and must not bypass existing protective functions.


### Electrical Constraints
---

The microcontroller and supporting electronics must operate within their specified voltage and current limits.

The expected boiler firing-rate input is a 0–10 V signal corresponding to approximately 0–100% firing demand. If the selected microcontroller uses a lower-voltage analog-to-digital converter, the firing-rate signal must be conditioned before reaching the controller.

The proposed temperature sensors use thermocouple technology and therefore require compatible measurement circuitry. The interface must account for the small thermocouple voltage, cold-junction compensation, and measurement accuracy.

The flow sensor interface must be compatible with the selected sensor's electrical output. Depending on the final sensor configuration, this may require a timer input, digital input, or additional signal-conditioning circuitry.

Electrical noise must also be considered because the controller may operate near pump motors, drives, and other electrical equipment. Signal filtering, appropriate grounding, cable routing, and separation of low-voltage control wiring from power wiring will be considered during the final design.

### Response Time and Stability Constraints
---

The controller must respond to changes in heating demand without producing unnecessary pump-speed fluctuations.

An excessively slow response could delay the system's adjustment to changing conditions. An excessively aggressive response could produce repeated pump-speed changes or undesirable temperature behavior.

The final sampling interval, control update interval, and adjustment limits will be selected based on sensor characteristics and measured system response.

The proposed controller must also avoid a significant increase in time-to-setpoint compared with baseline operation. The acceptable difference will be defined before final testing.


### Safety and Reliability Constraints
---

The controller must identify conditions that prevent reliable operation.

These conditions may include disconnected sensors, invalid measurements, insufficient water flow, loss of communication with the pump interface, controller restart, and loss of controller power.

Each critical condition must have a defined response.

The controller must not interfere with the boiler's existing safety protections. Any installation or testing involving electrical equipment and heated water must follow applicable laboratory procedures and manufacturer instructions.


### Standards, Ethical, and Economic Constraints
---

Electrical design and installation must consider applicable electrical safety requirements, including relevant provisions of NFPA 70 where applicable to the final installation.

Work involving energized equipment and hazardous energy must follow applicable laboratory procedures and OSHA requirements when those requirements govern the activity.

The design must also respect manufacturer-defined operating limits. Maintaining the required minimum circulation flow is both an equipment-protection requirement and an important engineering responsibility.

From an economic perspective, the controller should use reasonably priced hardware and avoid unnecessary components. The design should remain practical to assemble, maintain, troubleshoot, and test using available resources.

## Overview of Proposed Solution

Describe the solution and how it will fulfill the specifications and constraints of this subsystem.



The proposed solution uses a microcontroller to implement a demand-based pump-speed control algorithm with outlet-temperature feedback.

The control strategy will establish an initial pump-speed command using water flow rate and boiler firing rate. The controller will then evaluate the measured outlet temperature and determine whether the command requires adjustment.

The resulting pump-speed request will be checked against the allowable operating limits before being transmitted to the Pump and Motor Control subsystem.

### Microcontroller Hardware
---

An STM32-based microcontroller is a candidate implementation platform because it provides analog-to-digital conversion, timer peripherals, digital input/output, and serial communication capabilities.

These features could support the required sensor measurements, pump-speed output, and data logging interface.

The final microcontroller selection will depend on the actual sensor outputs, required pump interface, available input/output pins, power requirements, and software complexity.

### Sensor Data Acquisition
--- 
The microcontroller will acquire the required operating measurements and convert them into engineering units.

The flow sensor interface will convert the selected sensor output into GPM using the manufacturer's specified calibration relationship.

The firing-rate interface will convert the expected 0–10 V input into a percentage of firing demand.

For an ideal linear relationship:

[
F_{%}=\frac{V_{\text{firing}}}{10}\times100
]

The temperature measurement interfaces will provide the inlet and outlet water temperatures.

Before the measurements are used in the control algorithm, they will be checked against expected operating ranges.

Initial Pump-Speed Calculation

The initial pump-speed command will be calculated using the measured flow rate and boiler firing rate.

The relationship can be represented as:

[
u_{\text{initial}}=f(Q,F)
]

where:

(u_{\text{initial}}) is the initial pump-speed command.

(Q) is the measured flow rate.

(F) is the boiler firing-rate percentage.

(f(Q,F)) is the selected control relationship.

The final relationship may be implemented using a calibrated equation, lookup table, or another suitable control method.

The coefficients and operating limits must be determined using manufacturer information, simulation, and experimental measurements.

### Temperature Feedback
--- 

The initial pump-speed command will be adjusted according to the measured outlet-temperature error.

A general representation is:

[
u_{\text{requested}}=u_{\text{initial}}+\Delta u_T
]

where (\Delta u_T) is the temperature-feedback correction.

The correction must be based on the actual relationship between pump speed and temperature response.

A proportional controller, calibrated lookup relationship, or another control method may be selected after the system response has been evaluated.


### Pump Speed Limiting
--- 

The requested pump-speed command must remain within the allowable operating range.

The command can be limited using:

[
u_{\text{limited}}=
\min\left(u_{\max},
\max\left(u_{\min},u_{\text{requested}}\right)\right)
]

where (u_{\min}) and (u_{\max}) represent the permitted command limits.

The lower speed limit must not be treated as a substitute for flow measurement. The actual water flow must remain above the manufacturer-required minimum.


### Fault Handling
--- 

The controller will include operating states for initialization, sensor validation, normal operation, flow protection, fault handling, and shutdown or standby.

Organizing the program into separate states will simplify troubleshooting and allow each operating condition to be evaluated independently.

A fault response will be triggered when a critical measurement or interface becomes unreliable.

The final fault behavior must be coordinated with the selected pump and boiler equipment.


### Software Organization 
--- 

The control program will be divided into functions responsible for sensor acquisition, measurement conversion, input validation, control calculations, pump-output updates, fault handling, and data communication.

This structure will allow individual functions to be tested separately and modified as additional experimental information becomes available.



## Interface with Other Subsystems

Provide detailed information about the inputs, outputs, and data transferred to other subsystems. Ensure specificity and thoroughness, clarifying the method of communication and the nature of the data transmitted.





The Microcontroller and Control Logic subsystem communicates with the Sensor and Data Acquisition, Water Heating and Hydraulic, Pump and Motor Control, Data Logging and Simulation, and Monitoring and Performance Evaluation subsystems.


| Connected Subsystem | Direction | Information | Interface |
|---------------------|-----------|-------------|-----------|

| Data Logging and Simulation | Output | Measurements, commands, operating state, faults | Proposed serial communication |
| Monitoring and Performance Evaluation | Output | Recorded operating data | Shared analysis data |
| Power Supply | Input | Regulated electrical power | Selected supply connection |


### Sensor and Data Acquisition Interface 
--- 

The controller will receive the flow rate and temperature measurements from the selected sensors.

The proposed Keyence flow sensor must be connected using the electrical output supported by the exact sensor model.

If a pulse-frequency output is selected, a microcontroller timer or counter can be used to measure the pulse rate.

The inlet and outlet temperature sensors will require compatible thermocouple measurement circuitry.


### Water Heating and Hydraulic Interface
---

The expected boiler firing-rate signal will provide information about heating demand.

The 0–10 V input will be conditioned and converted into a firing-rate percentage.

The electrical characteristics and scaling relationship must be verified using the documentation for the selected Lochinvar equipment.


### Pump and Motor Control Interface
---

The controller will transmit a pump-speed command to the selected pump or drive.

The final electrical interface will depend on the equipment and may use an analog voltage, PWM signal, or digital communication method.

The command must remain within the permitted operating range.


### Data Logging and Simulation Interface
--- 

The microcontroller will transmit selected measurements and controller status information to the logging system.

The proposed record will include a timestamp, flow rate, inlet temperature, outlet temperature, firing rate, pump-speed command, operating state, and fault status.

The final communication format and transmission interval will be established after the hardware interface is selected.

### Enclosure Arrangement
--- 

The internal layout should provide adequate clearance around the controller and interface modules.

Connectors should be positioned so that external wiring can be installed and inspected without placing unnecessary stress on the electronics.

The arrangement should also provide access to programming and debugging connections.


### Mounting Considerations
---

The enclosure must support the weight of the installed electronics and provide secure mounting points.

The final mounting method must be appropriate for the intended installation environment.

Temperature exposure, moisture, vibration, and access for maintenance must be considered.
